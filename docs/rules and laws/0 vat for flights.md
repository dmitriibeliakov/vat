## Executive Summary (Dutch Customers)

| Aspect              | Treatment                                                  |
| :------------------ | :--------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Flight price**    | 0% VAT (pass-through, doorlopende post -- not our taxable amount) |
| **Our markup**      | 0% VAT (intermediation of international transport)         |
| **Markup disclosed on invoice?** | **No** -- since both the flight and our markup are 0%, the entire ticket price can be invoiced as a single line. Separately mentioning the margin is not obligatory. |

---

The zero-rated VAT (Value Added Tax) on international flights (flights to, from, or entirely outside the Netherlands) is a result of a "standstill" provision in European law that allows Member States to maintain historical exemptions.

In the Netherlands, this is codified in the **Wet op de omzetbelasting 1968** (VAT Act 1968).

### 1. Dutch National Legislation

The specific document that governs this is the **Wet op de omzetbelasting 1968**. Under Dutch law, the "zero rate" (0% VAT) is listed in **Table II (Tabel II)**, which is an integral part of the Act.

* **Document Name:** *Wet op de omzetbelasting 1968*
* **Article/Provision:** **Tabel II, onderdeel b, post 3**
* **Official Link:** [Wet op de omzetbelasting 1968 - Tabel II](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0002629/%23BijlageTabelII)
* **Explanation:** This post explicitly states that the zero rate applies to:

  > *"het vervoer van personen met zeeschepen of met luchtvaartuigen, mits het vervoer naar een bestemming buiten Nederland geschiedt dan wel van een herkomst buiten Nederland aanvangt;"*
  > *(The transport of persons by sea-going vessels or aircraft, provided the transport is to a destination outside the Netherlands or begins from a point of origin outside the Netherlands.)*

-----

### 2\. European Directive (The Legal Basis)

EU Member States are generally required to harmonize their VAT rates. However, the Netherlands is allowed to keep this 0% rate because it existed before 1978. This is permitted by the **EU VAT Directive**.

* **Document Name:** *Council Directive 2006/112/EC of 28 November 2006 on the common system of value added tax*
* **Article/Provision:** **Article 371** in conjunction with **Annex X, Part B, Point 10**
* **Official Link:** [Directive 2006/112/EC - EUR-Lex](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/%3Furi%3Dcelex%253A32006L0112)
* **Explanation:** \* **Article 371** allows Member States which exempted certain transactions on 1 January 1978 to continue to exempt them.
    - **Annex X, Part B, Point 10** specifically lists *"the transport of passengers"* as one of those transactions.
    - In the Netherlands, this "exemption" is technically applied as a **0% rate (zero-rating)**, which allows airlines to still deduct the VAT they pay on their own costs (like fuel or maintenance), making it a "VAT exemption with a right to deduct."

### Invoicing: Markup Does Not Need to Be Shown Separately

Because **both** the flight itself (Tabel II, post b.3) **and** the intermediation of the flight (Tabel II, post b.4) are subject to the same 0% rate, there is no obligation to disclose the markup or commission separately on the invoice. The entire ticket price can be shown as a single line at 0%.

This is confirmed by BDO (memo of 9 July 2025):

> *"For intermediary services with regard to flight tickets the current treatment of applying 0% is correct. As 0% applies on the whole ticket price separately mentioning the margin is not obligatory."*

And supported by the Dutch decree text:

> *"Het bemiddelen bij het sluiten van overeenkomsten tot het in de post bedoelde vervoer van personen valt ook onder het nultarief. [...] Als de bemiddeling op naam en voor rekening van derden plaatsvindt, valt de bemiddelingsdienst op grond van post b.4 onder het nultarief."*

**This is unique to flights.** For hotels and car rental, the underlying service and the intermediation carry different VAT rates, so the margin **must** be shown separately on the invoice.

---

**Note on Domestic Flights:** Flights that stay entirely within the Netherlands (e.g., Amsterdam to Maastricht, if it existed) do not fall under this zero rate and are subject to the standard Dutch VAT rate (currently 21%), as they do not meet the "international" criteria defined in the law.
