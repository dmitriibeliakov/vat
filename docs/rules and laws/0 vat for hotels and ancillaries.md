| Aspect              | Treatment                                                  |
| :------------------ | :--------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Hotel price**     | Pass-through (doorlopende post -- not our taxable amount). The hotel applies 21% VAT on accommodation, 9% on breakfast. |
| **Our markup**      | 21% VAT (standard rate, no reduced/zero rate for hotel intermediation) |

---

As of 1 January 2026, the Dutch VAT treatment of hotel accommodation has changed significantly. The reduced rate for **logies** (accommodation) was abolished, moving it from 9% to the standard 21% rate. Meanwhile, food services (such as breakfast) remain at the reduced 9% rate. This creates a split-rate situation when hotels bundle accommodation with breakfast.

C Teleport acts as a **disclosed agent** (bemiddelaar) for hotel bookings -- mediating in the name and for the account of the hotel. This means we do not buy and resell the hotel stay; we facilitate the transaction between customer and hotel. This distinction is critical for determining the applicable VAT regime.

---

## 1. Hotel Accommodation -- 21% Standard Rate (from 2026)

Until 31 December 2025, short-stay accommodation was subject to the reduced rate of 9%. From 1 January 2026, **Tabel I, onderdeel b, post 11** of the Wet op de omzetbelasting 1968 has been **abolished** via the Belastingplan 2026. Since accommodation is no longer listed in the reduced-rate table, the standard 21% rate applies automatically.

### Legal References

| Level        | Document                                     | Provision                                                              |
| :----------- | :------------------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Dutch**    | *Wet op de omzetbelasting 1968*              | **Tabel I, onderdeel b, post 11** (abolished 1 Jan 2026)              |
| **Dutch**    | *Belastingplan 2026*                         | Removes post b.11 from Tabel I                                        |
| **European** | *Directive 2006/112/EC*                      | **Annex III** (list of supplies eligible for reduced rates)            |

### What Counts as "Logies"

The abolished post b.11 covered:

> *"het verstrekken van logies binnen het kader van het hotel-, pension- en vakantiebestedingsbedrijf aan personen die daar slechts voor een korte periode verblijf houden"*
> *(The provision of accommodation within the framework of the hotel, boarding house and holiday accommodation business to persons who stay there only for a short period.)*

This includes: hotels, pensions, holiday cottages, static caravans, furnished seasonal rentals, and associated services directly linked to the stay (gas, electricity, sanitation, parking).

**Rate from 2026: 21%**

## 2. Breakfast and Food Services -- 9% Reduced Rate

Unlike accommodation, food and drink services consumed on premises remain at the reduced 9% rate. This is governed by two provisions in Tabel I:

* **Tabel I, onderdeel a, post 1** -- covers food products (goods/eet- en drinkwaren) at the reduced rate
* **Tabel I, onderdeel b, post 12** -- covers the provision of those food products for on-premises consumption within a hotel, cafe, restaurant, or similar establishment

### Legal References

| Level        | Document                                     | Provision                                                                |
| :----------- | :------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Dutch**    | *Wet op de omzetbelasting 1968*              | **Tabel I, onderdeel a, post 1** (food products)                        |
| **Dutch**    | *Wet op de omzetbelasting 1968*              | **Tabel I, onderdeel b, post 12** (on-premises food/drink in hotels)    |

**Rate: 9%**

## 3. Breakfast Is a Separate Supply from Accommodation

A critical question is whether breakfast bundled with a hotel stay should be treated as a single supply (ancillary to accommodation) or as two separate supplies with different VAT rates.

### ECJ Case Law

1. **ECJ Case C-349/96 (Card Protection Plan / CPP)** -- The foundational case on single vs. composite supplies. A service is "ancillary" if it does not constitute an aim in itself for the customer but a means of better enjoying the principal service. However, this must be assessed case by case.

2. **ECJ Case C-308/96 and C-94/97 (Madgett and Baldwin)** -- A service is ancillary when it contributes to the proper performance of the principal service, takes up a marginal proportion of the package, and is not sought by customers for its own sake.

3. **ECJ Joined Cases C-409/24, C-410/24, C-411/24 (2025)** -- Directly addresses hotel breakfast. The Advocate General concluded that **breakfast is NOT inseparable from the principal supply of accommodation**. Many hotels offer these services separately, indicating they are not inherently a single supply. Member States may tax breakfast at a different rate from accommodation.

### Dutch Practice from 2026

The Belastingdienst requires that when accommodation and breakfast are sold together at an all-in price, the price must be **split** into:

* An accommodation portion at **21%**
* A breakfast portion at **9%**

The split must be based on **market value proportions** ("marktwaarden van de afzonderlijke prestaties") and must be realistic. Implausible ratios (e.g., EUR 10 for room, EUR 190 for breakfast) will be challenged by tax authorities.

**Example from Belastingdienst:** A weekend stay for 2 persons at EUR 350 total is split into EUR 280 for accommodation (at 21%) and EUR 70 for breakfast (at 9%).

## 4. Our Role as Disclosed Agent (Bemiddelaar)

C Teleport acts as a **disclosed agent** -- we mediate in the name and for the account of the hotel. We do not buy the hotel service and resell it; we facilitate the transaction and collect payment on behalf of the hotel.

### Hotel Price Is a Pass-Through (Doorlopende Post)

Because we act as a disclosed agent, the hotel accommodation price we collect from the customer is a **doorlopende post** (pass-through item). This means the hotel price is **excluded from our taxable amount** -- we do not apply VAT to it. We only apply VAT to our own markup/commission.

The legal basis for this treatment:

**Artikel 4 Uitvoeringsbesluit omzetbelasting 1968** defines doorlopende posten:

> *"Tot de vergoeding behoren niet bedragen die de ondernemer van de afnemer heeft ontvangen ter voldoening van uitgaven die hij in naam en voor rekening van de afnemer heeft gedaan (doorlopende posten)."*
> *(Amounts received by the entrepreneur from the customer for expenditures incurred in the name and on account of the customer are not included in the taxable amount.)*

At the EU level, **Article 79(c) of Directive 2006/112/EC** provides:

> *"The taxable amount shall not include ... amounts received by a taxable person from the customer, as repayment of expenditure incurred in the name and on behalf of the customer."*

**Conditions** for the hotel price to qualify as a doorlopende post:
1. We act **in the name of** ("in naam van") the hotel or customer -- i.e., transparently as an intermediary
2. The payment is **on account of** ("voor rekening van") the hotel
3. The customer knows that the hotel service is provided by the hotel, not by C Teleport
4. The pass-through amounts are booked separately (not as our own revenue)

### VAT Treatment of Our Commission

Our own supply is a separate **intermediation service** (bemiddelingsdienst). Under **Artikel 8 Wet OB 1968**, VAT is calculated only on the "vergoeding" -- which for us is only the commission/markup. This commission is subject to the **standard 21% rate**, because intermediation for hotel services is not listed in any reduced-rate or zero-rate table.

**Important distinction from flights:** For international passenger transport, intermediation is zero-rated under Tabel II, onderdeel b, post 4 (intermediation related to the zero-rated services in posts b.1-b.3). No equivalent provision exists for hotel accommodation -- there is no reduced or zero rate for hotel intermediation.

### Why the "Buy and Resell" Fiction Does NOT Apply to Us

**Artikel 4, lid 4 Wet OB 1968** (implementing **Article 28 of Directive 2006/112/EC**) creates a fiction that an agent who acts **in his own name** (undisclosed/commissionair) is deemed to have both purchased and supplied the service. This would make the full hotel price part of the agent's taxable amount.

This fiction applies **only** to undisclosed agents acting "op eigen naam." As a disclosed agent (bemiddelaar) acting in the name of the hotel, we fall outside this provision entirely.

### The Travel Agency Margin Scheme (TOMS / Reisbureauregeling) Does NOT Apply

The TOMS applies only when a business acts **in its own name** toward the traveler and uses services purchased from other entrepreneurs. Since we act as a disclosed agent (not buying and reselling), TOMS is explicitly excluded.

## 5. Place of Supply for Intermediation (B2C)

For intermediary services provided to consumers (B2C), the place of supply follows the location of the underlying transaction, not the intermediary's establishment.

Since hotel accommodation is linked to immovable property, the intermediation service is taxed **where the hotel is located**:

* Hotel in the Netherlands + Dutch consumer = Dutch VAT on commission (21%)
* Hotel in another EU country + Dutch consumer = that country's VAT rules apply to the commission

| Level        | Document                                     | Provision                                  |
| :----------- | :------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------- |
| **Dutch**    | *Wet op de omzetbelasting 1968*              | **Artikel 6a**                             |
| **European** | *Directive 2006/112/EC*                      | **Article 46**                             |

For B2B customers, the general rule applies (Article 44 VAT Directive / Article 6 Wet OB): place of supply is where the customer is established.

## 6. Summary: What C Teleport Charges VAT On

As a disclosed agent, C Teleport does **not** apply VAT to the hotel price. The hotel price is a doorlopende post (pass-through). We only apply VAT to our own commission/markup.
